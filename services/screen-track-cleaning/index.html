Screens & tracks

Screen & Track Cleaning

The parts of a window nobody cleans until they stop working — screens, sills and the tracks the sashes run in.

Get a Free Quote ☎ 289-943-4395

Clean glass behind a grey screen still looks grey

A window screen is a filter, and it does its job. Everything it stops — road dust, pollen, grit off a gravel drive, wood smoke — stays in the mesh, dries there and builds a grey film that you look at every day without registering. Wash the glass and leave the screen and the window still looks dull, because you are not actually looking through clean glass.

Tracks fail differently. Grit collects at the bottom of every slider and casement channel, mixes with damp, and turns into a paste that makes a heavy patio door feel heavier every year. A light track clean does not rebuild anything, but it does get the channel clear so the sash runs the way it was meant to.

Cleaning, repair, or both

Most screens need a wash. Some need re-meshing. Which one you are looking at is usually obvious once a screen is out of the frame.

1

Out and inspected

Screens come out first. That is also when a tear, a bowed frame or a failed spline gets noticed.

2

Washed and dried

Both faces, then dried before they go back — a wet screen refitted against clean glass undoes the glass.

3

Repaired if needed

A torn screen can usually be re-meshed into its existing frame rather than replaced, which is cheaper and keeps the original fit.

4

Back where it came from

Refitted and checked that it seats and latches, not just pushed into the nearest opening.

A clean will fix

  • ✓Grey, dusty or pollen-clogged mesh
  • ✓Spider webbing in the frame corners
  • ✓Gritty tracks that make a slider stiff
  • ✓Sills full of debris and dead insects
  • ✓Screens that look fine until you compare one washed against one not

That is a repair, not a clean

  • •Tears, holes and mesh that has come away from the spline
  • •Frames bowed enough that the screen will not sit in the opening
  • •Missing corner keys or pull tabs
  • •Failed rollers or a track that is physically damaged
  • •Screens that were the wrong size before anyone touched them

Track work here is a clean. If a roller has gone or a channel is bent, we will tell you what we are seeing rather than clean around it and leave you to find out.

Re-meshing rather than replacing

A torn screen does not usually need a new screen. The frame is aluminium and outlasts the mesh several times over, so in most cases the old mesh comes out, the spline is replaced and new mesh goes into the frame you already have. It keeps the original fit — which matters, because a replacement screen ordered to a measurement is a coin toss on an older house.

Frames that are genuinely bent, corroded or missing their corner keys are the exception. We will say which category yours is in when we look at them, and it is worth having that conversation before ordering anything.
