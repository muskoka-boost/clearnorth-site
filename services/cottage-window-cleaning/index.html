Why cottage glass is its own job

A cottage window has a harder year than a suburban one. Pine and birch pollen arrives in a thick yellow wave in late spring and bakes on. Lake spray leaves mineral haze on anything facing open water. Spiders web the corners of every screen and soffit within reach of the shoreline, and boat and dock traffic keeps a fine grit in the air all summer. Then the place is shut for six months and none of it is touched.

The angle matters as much as the weather. Cottage glass is deliberately large and deliberately pointed at the water, which is exactly the direction the low morning and evening light comes from — so film that would be invisible on a north-facing kitchen window is the first thing you see from the sofa.

A cottage that is easy to describe is easy to quote. Photographs of the lake elevation and the approach save more back-and-forth than any other detail.

Getting a useful quote for a cottage

The quote form asks how many storeys the property has, whether basement windows are included, roughly how many windows there are, the approximate square footage and which services you want. Those five answers are most of the price. Anything you are not sure of can be left blank — an estimate is better than a guess, and we confirm before any work starts.

Add three things the form does not ask, because at a cottage they matter more than the window count: how the property is reached, whether anyone will be there, and the week you open or close. A cottage on a maintained township road with someone meeting us is a different job from a gated private lane with a key under a rock, even if the glass is identical.

Before an opening or closing visit

  • ✓Point out any window that was left open over winter
