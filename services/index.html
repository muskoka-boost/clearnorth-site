Glass, screens, sills, tracks — which is which

Four different jobs that happen at the same window, and the reason a quote asks about more than pane count.

Glass cleaning
The pane itself, washed and squeegeed by hand, inside and out. This is what most people mean by window cleaning.
Screen washing
Screens taken out, washed on both faces, dried and refitted. A grey screen makes clean glass look dirty, so this is not cosmetic.
Sill wiping
The ledge and frame below the glass, cleared of grit, dead insects and the debris that falls out when a screen comes off.
Light track cleaning
The channel a slider or casement runs in, cleared so the sash moves properly. It is a clean, not a repair — failed rollers and damaged channels are a different job, and we say so rather than clean around them.

What changes the price

There is no per-window rate, because two windows are rarely the same amount of work. The quote form asks how many storeys the property has, whether basement windows are included, roughly how many windows there are, the approximate square footage and which services you want — those five answers are most of the estimate.

Beyond that, three things move a number more than people expect. Access: a flat lawn on all four sides is quick, a deck, a steep grade or a side yard the width of a ladder is not. Condition: glass cleaned last spring takes a fraction of the time of glass that has not been touched in five years. Add-ons: gutters, soft washing or pressure washing on the same visit are priced together, because getting a crew and ladders to the property is the fixed cost either way.

Anything above three storeys is quoted case by case rather than off the form. Estimates are free, carry no commitment, and the price is confirmed before any work starts.
