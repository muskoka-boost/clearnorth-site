What a residential visit covers

A house has more kinds of glass than most people count. Front windows that face the road and collect traffic film, back windows over a deck that collect pollen and spider webbing, basement windows half-buried in a window well, and a patio door whose track has been quietly filling since it was installed. A residential visit takes all of it in one go rather than treating the obvious panes and leaving the rest.

The work is done by hand. Glass is washed and squeegeed, screens come out and are washed and dried before they go back, sills are wiped down and tracks get a light clean so the sashes run properly again. Nothing is pressure-washed at a window — pressure and seals are a bad combination.

Ask us before assuming

  • •Anything above three storeys — the quote form stops at three for a reason, and taller properties are priced case by case
  • •Hard-water staining, paint or mineral deposits that have been on the glass for years
  • •Storm windows and divided-light sashes, which take longer per pane
  • •Windows that cannot be reached safely from the ground or a ladder
  • •Interior glass in rooms nobody will be home to open

None of these are refusals — they are the things that change a price or need a look first, and we would rather flag them at the quote than on the day.

What affects the price

The quote form asks how many storeys the property has, whether basement windows are included, roughly how many windows there are, the approximate square footage and which services you want. Those five answers are most of the price. Anything you are not sure of can be left blank — an estimate is better than a guess, and we confirm before any work starts.

Two houses with the same square footage can quote very differently. A bungalow with sixteen large panes is quicker than a two-storey with forty small ones. Access matters too: a flat lawn on all four sides is straightforward, a deck, a steep grade or a fenced side yard the width of a ladder is not. Condition is the third factor — glass that has not been cleaned in five years takes longer than glass cleaned last spring.

Residential questions

Yes — interior and exterior glass are both part of a standard residential visit. You can also ask for exterior only, which some people prefer between full cleans.
Screen washing, sill wiping and light track cleaning are part of the residential service. Track work is a clean rather than a repair — if a roller has failed or a track is damaged, that is a different job and we will tell you rather than quietly leave it.
Someone needs to let us in for the interior glass. If you want exterior-only work done while you are out, say so when you ask for the quote so we can agree how that would work.
Exterior work is weather-dependent. Agree with us when you book how you would like a washout handled — some people would rather we came back the following week, others want the interior done anyway while we are there.
