Maintenance plans

Window Cleaning Maintenance Plans

A repeating schedule you agree once, so the glass never gets properly dirty — and you never have to remember to call.

Why a schedule costs less than a rescue

Glass that is cleaned regularly is quicker to clean. Film that has had one season to build comes off in a pass; film that has had five years is a different job with hard-water marks, baked pollen and organic staining underneath it. The first clean on a neglected property is nearly always the most expensive one it will ever have.

A plan is not a product with tiers and a brochure. It is an interval you choose and we keep to, so the visits happen without a phone call each time. What that interval should be depends entirely on the property — which is a conversation, not a dropdown.

How a plan works

  • ✓You choose the interval — seasonal, twice-yearly, quarterly, or something else
  • ✓The same scope each visit, agreed at the start
  • ✓Screens, sills and light track cleaning included as standard
  • ✓Other services can be attached to specific visits rather than all of them
  • ✓Changes and pauses are made by telling us, not by filling anything in
  • ✓Quoted per visit, so you can see it against a one-off

Worth being clear about

  • •There is no published pricing — every schedule is quoted for the property

  • •Anything about contracts, notice or billing should be settled when you set it up
  • •A one-off clean is a perfectly reasonable answer for many properties

If a plan is not right for your property we will say so. A schedule that does not suit the building is just a standing appointment nobody wanted.

Choosing an interval

There is no correct answer, only a correct answer for your building. Glass facing a busy road, a gravel driveway, farmland, a lake or a construction site will show film within a season. Sheltered glass facing a garden can go a year and still look fine.

The practical approach is to have one clean done, then look at the same windows two and four months later. Whichever point they start bothering you is your interval. Most residential properties land somewhere between twice a year and quarterly; commercial frontage is usually more often than that.

Keeping a plan running smoothly

  • ✓Tell us if access changes — a new gate code, a new dog, a new fence
  • ✓Say early if a visit needs moving, rather than on the day
  • ✓Flag anything that has changed on the property since the last visit
  • ✓Let us know if you want a service added to a particular visit
  • ✓Tell us if the interval is not working — it is meant to be adjusted
